Caring for patients
in ICE custody

Key action items — LMSA West / UCSF KYR Session, May 13, 2026

Section 1 of 3

At the bedside

  • ✨Remove physical restraints to perform a complete history, physical exam, and injury check
  • 🚪Ask ICE officers to step out for the entire history and exam; never allow them to serve as interpreters
  • 💬Use trained professional interpreters only
  • 📞Obtain verbal consent before calling emergency legal resources and the patient's emergency contact
  • 💊Anticipate missing medications (often confiscated at intake) — factor into disposition planning
  • 🧠Screen for PTSD, depression, anxiety, and social needs; prepare referrals for mental health, housing, and food
Section 2 of 3

Escalation & documentation

  • ⚠️Do not handle ICE encounters alone — escalate to your supervising resident, attending, or on-duty administrator
  • 📛Collect visible badge numbers and identification of any ICE officers present
  • ⚖️Consult legal/risk management for any subpoena or records request; avoid documenting immigration status when possible
  • 🔒Share only stability and disposition information with officers — nothing beyond what HIPAA requires
Section 3 of 3

Institutional advocacy

  • 📒Compile local legal organization contacts (names, numbers, best contact methods) before a custody patient arrives
  • 📱Save the Google Voice rapid-response number to your phone — real-time legal support during ICE encounters, including outside business hours
  • 📄Familiarize yourself with the People's Care Collective model hospital policy; cite peer institutions (UCSF, UCLA, Hennepin) when advocating locally
  • 🤝Build a departmental coalition and identify an ally to help move policy through leadership
